Compliance

Built for Indian regulations

CERT-In, the DPDP Act, RBI, SEBI and IRDAI all expect you to control privileged access and prove it. Vaultgate gives you the controls they ask for and the evidence your auditors check, on an appliance in your own data centre.

CERT-InDPDP ActRBISEBIIRDAI ISO 27001Data residencyYour part

Regulations apply to your organisation, not to a product, and no regulator certifies PAM tools. What follows maps each framework's privileged access expectations to what Vaultgate does. It is a guide for your compliance team, not legal advice: check the current text of each regulation, which regulators revise.

CERT-In Directions (April 2022)

Issued under section 70B of the IT Act and binding on service providers, intermediaries, data centres and body corporates in India.

What it asksHow Vaultgate helps
Keep logs of all ICT systems for 180 days, within IndiaThe audit log is never deleted. Session recordings can't be set to less than 180 days. Everything stays on the appliance in your own data centre
Synchronise clocks to NIC or NPL time servers, or to servers traceable to themTime page: set NTP servers from the console (one click for NIC and NPL), see the offset and the servers in use, and get an alert if the clock loses sync
Report incidents within 6 hoursAlerts by email as things happen, every audit event forwarded to your SOC at once, and the full trail of who did what, ready for the report
Provide logs to CERT-In on requestExport the audit log as CSV, or produce a PDF report for any period. Recordings can be exported as evidence packages

Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Rules 2025

Applies to every organisation processing digital personal data in India. Most duties under the Rules take effect in phases after November 2025.

What it asksHow Vaultgate helps
Reasonable security safeguards, including access controlNobody needs to know server passwords: Vaultgate holds them encrypted, injects them into sessions and changes them after use. Access is by policy, approval and time window
Visibility of access to personal data, to detect and investigate unauthorised accessEvery privileged session is recorded, with typed commands for SSH and keystrokes for remote desktops. Sign-ins, approvals and password reveals are all audited
Keep the logs for at least one yearThe audit log is kept for the life of the appliance. Set recordings to 365 days or more, or keep them forever
Notify the Board and affected people of a breachThe recorded sessions and audit trail show exactly which systems were reached, by whom and when, which scopes the notification

Reserve Bank of India

Banks, NBFCs, payment system operators and co-operative banks: the Master Direction on IT Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices (2023), the Cyber Security Framework for banks, and the IT outsourcing direction.

What it asksHow Vaultgate helps
Privileged access management: vaulted and rotated administrator passwordsVault with AES-256-GCM envelope encryption; automatic rotation for servers, databases and network devices; passwords changed after each use
Multi-factor authentication for privileged usersAuthenticator codes that administrators can require, and single sign-on with your own identity provider
Monitoring and recording of privileged activitySSH, RDP and VNC sessions recorded and searchable; live monitoring; sessions ended on blocked commands or keywords
Least privilege and need-based, time-bound accessApproval workflows with time windows; personal just-in-time accounts created for a session and removed after it
Periodic review of privileged accessAn auditor role that can see and report but not change anything; period reports of users, access, usage and findings
Logs to the bank's SOCEvery audit event forwarded to your SIEM over syslog, encrypted and in order
Right to audit the vendor and continuity of critical servicesA self-hosted appliance under your control, with standby and automatic failover, encrypted backups and an offline recovery kit

Your data stays in your network

Wherever you run Vaultgate, in India or anywhere else, everything it holds stays on your own appliance. That is what makes data residency rules straightforward to meet.

DeploymentAn appliance on your own hardware or hypervisor, in your own data centre. No cloud service
What leaves the applianceNothing you don't send: email alerts to your mail server, events to your SIEM, backups to where you put them. The daily license check-in sends only the appliance's identity, version and usage counts, and offline activation removes it
EvidenceRecordings and keystroke logs are encrypted on the appliance, the audit log is tamper-evident, and exports are limited to people you allow and audited

What stays your part

Policies and reviewsDeciding who should have access, and reviewing it on schedule. Vaultgate enforces and records it
Incident reportingReporting to CERT-In, the Data Protection Board or your regulator. Vaultgate gives you the facts quickly
Retention settingsChoosing recording retention beyond the 180-day minimum to match your regulator and your own policy
Everything outside privileged accessEndpoint, network and application controls, and the rest of your security programme
